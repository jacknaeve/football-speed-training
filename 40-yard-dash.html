How to Run a Faster 40-Yard Dash

The 40-yard dash rewards a strong start more than raw top speed. Most players can cut tenths of a second by fixing the first ten yards, then staying relaxed through the finish.

Step 1: Set your stance

Use a three-point stance with your weight forward on your hand and front foot. Your hips should sit slightly higher than your shoulders, and your back stays flat. Stay still until you go, because early movement wastes time.

Step 2: Drive out low

Push the ground behind you for the first five to six steps. Keep your head down and your body at a forward lean, and let your torso rise gradually. Short, powerful steps beat long reaching ones here.

Step 3: Transition to upright running

Between yards 10 and 25, stand up slowly and let your stride lengthen. Swing your arms from hip to cheek with your elbows bent at about 90 degrees.

Step 4: Finish through the line

Keep your face and shoulders loose. Sprint past the finish instead of slowing early, and do not lunge, since lunging breaks your rhythm.

Common mistakes

  • Standing up too early in the first five steps
  • Overstriding with long, reaching steps
  • Tensing the neck and shoulders
  • Running too many full 40s when tired
